What the schedule does

This is the fatigue index's report card. Every schedule spot the board scores, with how the team actually did against the book's team total. "Book" is the implied team total from the closing spread and total; "Miss" is actual points minus that, so a negative miss means the book had the team too high. Under the line at 52.4% or better pays at −110. "Floor" is the 95% lower bound on the under rate, so a spot whose floor clears 50% is a signal rather than noise. "Game under" is the full-game total.

Does the projection beat the book?

Edge is the book's number minus the board's projection. Positive edge means the book has the team (or game) higher than we do, so the under is the play. If the projection knows nothing the book doesn't, every row sits near 50%. At −110 you need 52.4% to break even, and the floor column tells you whether a bucket's hit rate could be luck.

Which inputs predict points, and which predict what the market missed

Each input tested on its own. "vs points" is its correlation with points scored: offense and pace score high here, but the book already prices season ratings, so that column mostly tells you what the market knows. "vs market miss" is the correlation with points minus the book line: that is the column that matters, because it is the only thing you can bet on. Basketball is noisy game to game, so 0.03–0.06 against the market is a real signal and 0.10 is large. The right column compares the fifth of games where the input most favoured the under with the fifth where it least did.
